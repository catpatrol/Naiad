## §4 · WHAT THE STUDY YEARS TAUGHT THAT TRANSFERS (carry these as law)
**Edge before structure** — no entity, tool, or optimization before the underlying activity
demonstrably works (the fee-restructure rule, generalized). **Costs are scale-invariant** — the
toll lesson: fees, taxes, subscriptions, and drag compound exactly like edge, and are the only
part of the future you control with certainty; audit them first. **Falsification is a
deliverable** — a plan without a stated failure condition is a wish; write "what proves me
wrong" into every projection. **Outlier honesty** — the ZEC lesson: when one client, one asset,
one month is most of the result, *say so on the face of the table*; concentration is the first
risk and the last thing anyone admits. **The elegant simplicity of that which arrives without
moving** — prefer the rule that is a refusal: the standing order, the automatic transfer, the
default that needs no willpower. **And the hazard-curve habit** — for any position (financial or
business) know, before entering, what deterioration to what level means for the odds; decide
exit language while calm.
