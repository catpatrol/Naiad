What changed, and why

Four corrections. None of them altered a single stored number; all of them altered a reading. They are listed first because you should carry them into every chart below.

1 · The outcome horizon was wrong by 12× to 288×

The forward-outcome column is a rolling window over the lens's own bars, anchored at the pivot's confirmation bar. Per lens: 1H → 100 hours (4 days) · 4H → 400 hours (17 days) · 12H → 1,200 hours (50 days) · 1D → 2,400 hours (100 days). v2 said: "the outcome is measured over the 100 bars (8.3 hours) after the pullback."

2 · Pooled outcome columns mix horizons

Every chart that pools termini across lenses puts 4-day and 100-day outcomes in one column. The sequence table, the depth cut and the frontier are all mixtures. Where it matters, v2.1 now shows a single-lens control alongside the pooled view.

3 · The look-ahead audit had the right conclusion for the wrong reason

v2 said wider counting windows credit crosses that fired after the outcome was decided. They don't — the outcome window is far longer than that. They credit crosses that fire during the move being measured, and after the decision curtain (the moment the pivot becomes knowable: 5 lens bars past the low). Contemporaneity, not hindsight. The conclusion — reason with the causal cut — stands, and v2.1 adds the curtain cut that makes it precise.

4 · "The gradient holds within each lens" was false

It holds in the 1H lens (+0.49 ATR, n≈10.6k) and reverses in the 4H lens; the 12H and 1D lenses lack the sample to say. The pooled gradient is also partly a composition effect — slower frontier categories contain more slow-lens, longer-horizon termini. The finding survives, narrowed to one lens, with the per-asset replication intact.

What did not change: the ladder and its transition probabilities, the timing heatmaps, the gap distributions and the inter-rung tempo. All are counts and lags with no outcome dependence — the 1H→4H stall, the page's central finding, is untouched.

How to read it

The staircase is the cascade as a clock. The 5m band hugs t=0 on both sides; each slower frame steps outward on both sides in near-regular log steps. A pullback is the hinge of a temporally ordered structure: the fast stack has just turned, the slow stack turned long ago and will not speak again for a long time.

Across the toggles

The 89/200 stage clock sits about one log-step further out than the regime clock on every frame — structure moves later than regime, uniformly. The 9/200 sits between them, consistent with its measured role as the mid-sequence marker (canonical in 97% of chains). Same machine, three gears.

Transition detail

Tight and adjacent while fast: 5m→15m 84%, 15m→30m 80%. It loosens at 30m→1H (63%), then breaks: from 1H the cascade falls back to 30m (48%) more often than it advances to 4H (29%). That stall is the structure the whole page is about.

Three readings [all OPEN]

  • A participation cascade. Fast frames turn on small flow; slower frames need sustained flow. 80%+ adjacent advance below 1H says small moves routinely propagate two or three rungs; 29% at 1H→4H says only a minority recruit enough participation to turn the 4-hour trend. On this reading the 4H cross is the census's operational definition of a move that recruited real size.
  • Where trends die young. The 47.5% fall-back from 1H is the fast stack oscillating under a slow stack that never moved — most cascades are round-trips of the fast frames. This is the anatomy behind the re-entry toll bleed: fast-rung churn generates fills whose moves rarely graduate.
  • The engine sees a coarser ladder. Its telemetry is {5m, 1H, 4H, 12H} — no 15m or 30m rungs. The dominant observed path 5m→15m→30m is invisible churn from the engine's seat; the first engine-visible confirmation above exec is 1H, and the first that historically means anything is 4H.

Applications for consideration: condition adds on "cleared 1H→4H" rather than any fast-rung event · treat fast-rung activity as churn to filter, not signal to chase · read the 4H rung as the participation threshold D8 already priced (1.071 net of toll) — and note it is 4H-specific: the 12H rung is 0.809, far below break-even. Late confirmation is spent confirmation.

Corrected · read this one

Does reaching a higher rung mean a bigger move?

Yes — but less than the naive cut suggests, and the honest version needs two controls v2 lacked. Control one: the decision curtain. A (5,5) pivot is confirmed five lens bars after the low; only crosses at or before that instant were knowable. Control two: the lens. Pooling mixes 4-day and 100-day outcome windows, so the single-lens view is the one with a constant yardstick.

What the toggles show

Why it matters. On the 1H lens with a constant yardstick, the causal cut shows 3.94 → 4.28 at 4H. The ≤48h cut shows 6.60 at 4H. The gap is information that arrives after the decision — real, but untradeable. The curtain cut sits between them and is the maximum a decision could legitimately use; its deep cells are thin, because only five hours have elapsed.

Interpretations [all OPEN]

  • State beats event. This gradient measures a state — how deep the already-standing alignment reaches — and echoes the confirmed D5 entry. Contrast the event axis: waiting for the 12H cross to fire and adding there scores 0.809 in D8, a heavy loser, because that cross arrives ~83 hours into the move. The census compresses to one sentence: trade the state of the slow frames; act on the event of the 4H frame.
  • A maturity sweet spot, not a monotone. The gradient rises to 4H–12H and flattens; the daily adds nothing beyond 12H. Deep-enough alignment marks a trend mature enough to trust; beyond that, extra depth is redundant information about the same trend. It is also why the engine's missing 1D frame costs the findings almost nothing.
  • The skeptical alternative, now partly borne out. Deep alignment is commoner in regimes that trended hard, so some of the gradient may be regime confound rather than per-trade signal. The 4H lens reversing is exactly what that objection predicts. The per-asset panel below is the counter-evidence, and it survives the horizon control.

Reading the two orderings against each other

By share, 5m→15m→30m dominates (60.6%, a middling 4.44). By outcome the top rows touch 4H or 12H within three rungs — each under 2.5% of cascades. The market's most common behaviour and its most profitable behaviour are nearly disjoint sets.

The frontier, pooled — for continuity only

v2's headline chart, retained so the correction is visible rather than hidden. This is the horizon-mixture view; the per-lens table above is the one to reason with.

Pooled, alignment reaching 4H–12H is where the gradient appears. Roughly half that spread is the lens-composition effect; the rest survives in the 1H lens.

What this is not. Not a validated finding, not a trading rule, not out-of-sample, and not net-of-cost — this substrate has no adverse-excursion data, so every outcome is upside-only. Every figure is computed in-sample on exploration-classic data that has already been searched.

The outcome column, precisely. Median forward maximum-favorable excursion in ATR, measured over 100 bars of the terminus's own lens timeframe, anchored at the pivot's confirmation bar (five lens bars after the low). 1H lens → 4 days · 4H → 17 days · 12H → 50 days · 1D → 100 days. Any chart pooling lenses mixes these windows.

A labelling defect in the substrate, disclosed. Terminus rows are timestamped at the pivot bar, not at the confirmation bar where the pivot becomes knowable. No stored feature or outcome depends on that timestamp — features are read at the pivot bar and outcomes from the confirmation bar — so no published number is affected. It matters here only because the clock's t=0 is that pivot bar, which is why the decision curtain is drawn.

Engine-representability. The engine's world is {1m, 5m, 15m, 1h, 4h, 12h} with MTF telemetry on {5m, 1h, 4h, 12h}. The 30m and 1D frames are resampled measurement lenses with no engine counterpart; results anchored on them are diagnostic. The census's four "governor lenses" are measurement frames — the engine's governors are 1H intraday, 4H swing, 12H position.
